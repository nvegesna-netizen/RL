\begin{abstract}
Asynchronous reinforcement learning overlaps rollout generation and policy updates, but release delay can cause a rollout to miss a gradient opportunity before reaching the learner. We introduce M4, a controlled-release instrument that measures pre-release opportunity, randomizes an added zero- or five-second delay, and audits terminal delivery and version advance. An initial acquisition validated the instrument and mechanism, but its interval crossed a prespecified 0.20 materiality threshold; a prospectively redesigned follow-up lay entirely above it. Six definitive Qwen3-by-math-workload cells contain 49,153 assignments: five intervals are above the threshold, while one positive interval is entirely below it. A dependency-aware synthesis preserves shared GSM8K anchors and finds heterogeneous model-scale effects. Prospective Llama 3.2 1B and 3B extensions add eight acquisitions. Both 1B workload intervals are above 0.20; at 3B, the OpenMath interval is above it and the GSM8K interval crosses it. Prespecified 3B-minus-1B contrasts are negative on both workloads. Across 14 acquisitions and 106,653 assignments, the results establish a positive, heterogeneous opportunity-loss effect that exceeds the prespecified threshold in most tested systems, not final-quality, pure-family, or general scaling claims.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Asynchronous LLM reinforcement learning improves accelerator utilization by overlapping rollout generation and policy optimization. The overlap also makes experience time-dependent: while a rollout waits, the learner may advance or fill the near-term window that could have consumed it. A delayed rollout can contain useful signal yet lose its opportunity to contribute to a gradient update.

Observational latency comparisons do not identify that cost. Difficult prompts may both run longer and produce different rewards or gradient opportunity, while batching, load, and model scale jointly affect latency and cadence. We therefore introduce M4, a randomized controlled-release instrument. For each eligible epoch-specific group, M4 records pre-release opportunity $Q$, assigns immediate release or an added five-second hold, and observes terminal delivery. Its estimand is the treatment-induced change in lost opportunity normalized by pre-delay opportunity.

Opportunity loss is distinct from the usual age or staleness of a consumed trajectory. Staleness describes how far the learner has advanced by the time an example is used. M4 instead asks whether a controlled release delay destroys a concrete near-term chance for an otherwise eligible group to enter training. A delayed group may ultimately be consumed at a later version, be removed without contributing, or be replaced by other ready work. Those outcomes have different learning implications even when their wall-clock delay is identical. Measuring the opportunity before release is therefore essential: defining it afterward would allow the treatment itself to change which opportunities are counted.

This distinction matters to system design. A scheduler cannot infer a universal safe delay merely from throughput or average trajectory age. It needs to know how delay interacts with buffer occupancy, update cadence, and the opportunity distribution in its particular execution. M4 provides that causal diagnostic. It deliberately does not assume that every lost opportunity translates into an accuracy decrement. We test that downstream link separately with independently randomized training runs and retain its inconclusive result.

The evidence was accumulated sequentially. A 224-step acquisition established instrument operation and replicated the proposed version-advance mechanism, but its interval crossed the registered materiality threshold. A new design then froze balanced assignment, pre-treatment adjustment, twice the training horizon, and an outcome-blind power gate. The follow-up interval lay entirely above the threshold. Model and workload transports and a prospectively paired third workload completed a $2\times3$ map. Subsequent four-acquisition Llama studies prospectively tested family and size transport within two workloads.

We contribute (i) a protocol-bound causal instrument for opportunity loss; (ii) a prospective sequence that separates pipeline completion, mechanism support, and causal materiality; (iii) a six-cell Qwen grid and eight Llama acquisitions totaling 106,653 assignments with complete terminal scoring; and (iv) dependency-aware Qwen heterogeneity and prespecified Llama size contrasts without overstating shared or fixed factors.

\section{Related Work}
GRPO was introduced for mathematical reasoning in DeepSeekMath~\cite{shao2024deepseekmath}. AReaL~\cite{fu2025areal}, PipelineRL~\cite{piche2025pipelinerl}, and DORA~\cite{hu2026dora} develop asynchronous LLM-RL systems and mechanisms for efficiency, staleness control, or data integrity. Other work analyzes staleness scaling~\cite{song2026staleness} or changes optimization using gradient alignment and adaptive trust regions~\cite{xu2026gac,yang2026stale}. RollPacker~\cite{gao2025rollpacker} and TailSieve~\cite{xu2026tailsieve} target long-tail rollout scheduling.

M4 addresses a narrower measurement problem. It does not propose an optimizer or scheduler. It perturbs release time after measuring opportunity and estimates the causal destruction of normalized gradient opportunities. Randomization separates controlled delay from naturally slow examples, while event ledgers and observer-duty audits make the systems path inspectable.

The distinction in evaluation scope is important. AReaL, PipelineRL, and DORA pair system designs with speed, convergence, or final-performance comparisons, while TailSieve evaluates a concrete routing policy. M4 instead validates a measurement instrument and maps its response across fixed executions. Consequently, the present evidence establishes a causal systems cost but not a throughput--quality frontier or the benefit of a production scheduling policy. We report that boundary explicitly rather than treating the proximal endpoint as model quality.

\section{Setting and Estimand}
All definitive experiments use single-controller GRPO in NeMo RL~\cite{nvidia2025nemorl}, asynchronous vLLM generation, and two H100 GPUs in one scheduled workload. The Qwen synthesis uses Qwen3-0.6B and Qwen3-1.7B~\cite{qwen2025qwen3} on OpenMath~\cite{toshniwal2024openmath}, GSM8K~\cite{cobbe2021gsm8k}, and NuminaMath~\cite{numinamath2024dataset}. Prospective extensions use Llama 3.2 1B and 3B on OpenMath and GSM8K, twice per size/workload. The assignment unit is an epoch-specific prompt group with eight sibling generations.

Let $A_i\in\{0,1\}$ indicate control or added five-second delay, $Q_i\ge0$ be opportunity recorded before release, and $D_i$ indicate that the opportunity was not delivered. The finite-sample unadjusted contrast is
\begin{equation}
\Delta_L=\frac{E[Q D\mid A=1]-E[Q D\mid A=0]}{E[Q\mid A=0]}.
\end{equation}
The registered primary estimator is a cross-fitted augmentation of this randomized contrast using only $Q$ and $1[Q=0]$ as pre-treatment covariates, eight contiguous start-version folds, and pooled pre-delay normalization. Positive values mean added delay destroys more opportunity. Because the denominator is pooled pre-delay opportunity, $\Delta_L=0.20$ means that the arm contrast in lost opportunity equals one fifth of the average opportunity available before treatment; it is not a 20-point accuracy loss. The $0.20$ decision threshold was fixed in the analysis implementation before the first causal outcome and retained in every transport. The recovered pre-outcome record does not contain an external utility derivation for that value. We therefore report continuous estimates and threshold sensitivity, call $0.20$ a prespecified decision threshold, and do not retrofit a learning-quality interpretation. ``Not material'' does not mean a zero causal effect.

Terminal missingness is bounded by adversarial endpoint assignment, with at most 1\% allowed per arm. Dependence is indexed by start version. We use four-lag Newey--West HAC inference~\cite{newey1987hac} and a 20,000-draw circular-block bootstrap with eight-version blocks~\cite{politis1992circular}. The cell envelope is the union of both intervals across missingness endpoints. All definitive terminal dispositions are observed.

\section{Instrument and Prospective Sequence}
M4 combines a common opportunity ledger, randomized controlled release, terminal bounds, detached protocol-bound inference, and corrected observer-duty measurement. Mechanism checks test whether delay breaks the direct release-to-train chain and increases learner-version advance. Source, protocol, configuration, and terminal artifacts are SHA-256 bound.

For each group, the lifecycle is: reserve an identity and starting policy version; assign the release arm from a frozen domain; complete all sibling generations; measure the eligible pre-release opportunity; apply the assigned hold; admit the group to the ready buffer; and record whether it is selected, evicted, stale, or otherwise removed. Learner-version advances are written into the same monotonic clock domain. The detached analyzer joins these facts only after terminal execution, reconstructs the delivery indicator, and rejects incomplete or contradictory histories. Assignment, opportunity measurement, and terminal classification are thus separate auditable stages rather than fields produced by one outcome-aware callback.

The first 224-step acquisition scored all 3,298 primary assignments. Direct-chain rates were 0, 0.267, and 0.586 for control, d5, and a d10 positive-control arm; mean version advances were 0, 0.437, and 0.805. The dose ordering supported the mechanism. The primary d5 estimate was 0.210 with envelope $[0.116,0.302]$, which crossed the prespecified 0.20 threshold.

That completed artifact informed design, not follow-up outcomes. The redesigned study removed d10, balanced control and d5, froze the adjusted estimator, and doubled training to 448 steps. A 20,000-draw version-cluster simulation estimated 0.816 power under a prospective alternative of 0.25, above the frozen 0.80 gate. The Qwen3-0.6B/OpenMath follow-up scored 7,199 assignments and estimated 0.305 with envelope $[0.276,0.335]$, entirely above 0.20. No outcome-guided extension occurred.

One-axis transports then changed model scale on OpenMath and workload to GSM8K. A prospectively designed Qwen3-0.6B/GSM8K cell completed the $2\times2$ grid. Finally, both NuminaMath protocols were frozen and submitted as a pair before either outcome was inspected. These cells supply a third workload while reusing immutable GSM8K anchors for the registered comparison.

After the Qwen grid closed, separate Llama 3.2 1B and 3B protocols each froze two independently randomized 400-version acquisitions for OpenMath and GSM8K. Each run retained the accepted estimator and uncertainty procedure; workload endpoints used equal replicate weight and block-diagonal covariance. Qualification observations did not enter the estimator, and no outcome-guided extension occurred.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
The study supports three scoped conclusions: controlled release delay causes opportunity loss in the tested asynchronous GRPO cells; the loss is operationally material in most but not all cells; and the model-scale contrast varies across workloads. A universal delay tolerance would therefore obscure relevant runtime context.

M4 measures a proximal systems consequence. Missing one gradient opportunity need not change final reward if later data compensate, while repeated loss of high-opportunity groups could compound. The separate run-randomized study measured terminal task accuracy, but its interval crossed zero and both registered relevance bounds; the direction and practically relevant magnitude of the accuracy difference therefore remain unresolved. Convergence and final reward remain untested. The proximal design covers two small Qwen3 models and two Llama 3.2 sizes, three math workloads, GRPO, one primary nonzero delay, two H100 GPUs, and one cluster environment. Model, family, and workload are fixed rather than randomized factors. Distinct acquisition runs are treated as independent; unrecorded cluster-wide conditions could create residual dependence. NuminaMath is a prospective same-direction extension with a shared reference, and Llama supplies bounded family and size transport rather than causal family or scaling contrasts.

The downstream experiment therefore used a different unit of randomization. The within-run control and d5 groups update one shared policy and cannot yield arm-specific final models, so the end-to-end study randomized whole training runs, froze held-out evaluation and budget, and analyzed matched seed blocks. This identifies the total effect of the release regime in that Qwen3-0.6B/OpenMath setting. Even a more precise result would not, without mediation assumptions, prove that opportunity loss is the only path from delay to quality because delay can also alter ordering and staleness. The observed between-run SD of 0.167 further limits precision and transport.

The Qwen3-0.6B/GSM8K acquisition also illustrates why pipeline status is not a scientific endpoint. Its pipeline completed all registered training and preserved immutable ledgers, then failed while invoking an incompatible post-run analyzer. Applying the preregistered correct analyzer without new data or protocol changes recovered its canonical result. We label the pipeline failed and the acquisition scientifically complete.

\section{Reproducibility and Conclusion}
Every acquisition binds source, protocol, raw ledgers, and terminal archive by SHA-256. Qualification data never enter estimators; failures remain documented; automatic retry and outcome-guided extension were prohibited. The supplement reports source commits, pipeline states, artifact hashes, observer duty, software identity, and offline robustness. Large raw artifacts remain outside Git pending anonymous access review.

The evidence sequence is: validated instrument $\rightarrow$ replicated mechanism $\rightarrow$ threshold-crossing initial interval $\rightarrow$ prospectively redesigned confirmation $\rightarrow$ cross-model/workload heterogeneity $\rightarrow$ prospective same-direction workload extension $\rightarrow$ prospectively replicated cross-family extension $\rightarrow$ prospective within-family size extension $\rightarrow$ prospective end-to-end quality test. Five of six Qwen3 intervals lie above 0.20; both Llama 3.2 1B intervals do, while at 3B only the OpenMath interval lies wholly above it. Prespecified size contrasts support attenuation in both tested fixed configurations. The downstream accuracy estimate is negative but insufficiently precise to establish its direction and practically relevant magnitude. This supports a causal systems-measurement result without claiming a final-quality effect, equivalence, pure family effects, general scaling laws, or broader generalization.
